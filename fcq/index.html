Under the Hood

Why the search looks where it does.

Multiplying two primes takes a computer microseconds. Splitting their product back apart has taken thousands of CPU-years, and the largest RSA numbers remain unfactored. RSA encryption relies on that gap. Factoring Challenge Quest lets you feel it directly.

The ceiling

p ≤ q ⇒ p ≤ ⌊√N⌋

Call the smaller factor p. Since p × p ≤ p × q = N, the smaller prime can never exceed the integer square root of N.

The floor

bits(p) ≈ bits(N) / 2

RSA challenge primes were generated at similar sizes, so p has about half of N's bits. The app starts one bit lower as a safety margin.

The sieve

3 · 5 · 7 · … · 509

Any candidate divisible by an odd prime below 512 can't be P, so it's skipped before the expensive big-number division.

Test your own prime

N mod p = 0 ?

Have a favorite prime? Type it in, and the app shows its remainder against N. Every technical term in the app explains itself with a tap.

Straight Talk

No catch.

Completely free

No ads, no in-app purchases, no accounts. Every feature is available from the first launch.

Real work, honest odds

Every attempt is a genuine division against the official modulus. The app never pretends you are close: random trial division will not break a modern RSA number, and it says so.

Private by design

Your runs and totals stay on your device. The app only downloads public files from mathricks.com, such as the challenge catalog, and never uploads anything.
