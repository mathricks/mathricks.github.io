Plain-English guide

What Is a
Bitcoin Puzzle?

The numbered Bitcoin/BTC puzzles are public, funded cryptographic challenges built from deliberately limited private-key ranges. They are a demonstration of scale, not a flaw in Bitcoin.

Published 29 July 2026 About 7 minutes to read

The concise answer

A public hunt for deliberately constrained keys.

A Bitcoin puzzle, often called a BTC puzzle, is a public cryptographic challenge in which bitcoin was placed at addresses whose private keys were deliberately chosen from limited, known ranges. Anyone can test candidate keys. Solving a puzzle means finding the private key that controls one of those funded addresses.

Each numbered level has one target address and one defined interval. If the target is Puzzle n, its private key k lies in the range:

2n−1 ≤ k < 2n

There is no word riddle or hidden clue to decode. The challenge is to locate one unknown number inside a known but rapidly expanding interval, derive its Bitcoin address, and match that address to the published target.

The name “puzzle” has stuck, but “bounded private-key search challenge” is the more literal description.

The level system

Why every number gets harder.

A level number describes the size of its private-key interval. Moving up one level doubles the number of candidates, so the difficulty grows exponentially rather than in equal steps.

01

A fixed interval

Puzzle 1 contains one candidate. Puzzle 2 contains two. Puzzle 3 contains four. The same rule continues through the higher levels.

02

Twice the search space

Puzzle 71 contains 270 candidates—about 1.18 sextillion. Puzzle 72 contains twice as many.

03

A funded target

The creator distributed ~32 BTC in bitcoin across the published target addresses initially. Later their top-ups raised the total to ~1000 BTC, which is why the challenge became known as the "~1000 BTC challenge" rather than the original "~32 BTC."

How puzzles are searched

One challenge, two very different cases.

The available public information determines which kind of search is possible. This is why some higher-numbered puzzles can be more practical targets than lower-numbered ones.

Address known

Candidate-by-candidate search

When only a Bitcoin address and its interval are known, hunters generate candidate private keys, derive their addresses, and compare the results with the target. No generally useful shortcut is known for these deliberately random-looking ranges.

Work grows with the range approximately 2n−1
Public key exposed

Square-root discrete-log search

Spending from a traditional puzzle address reveals its public key. Once that happens, interval algorithms such as Pollard’s kangaroo can search for the corresponding private key in roughly the square root of the interval size.

Work grows with the square root approximately 2(n−1)/2

Revealing a public key does not merely cut the work “in half.” For Puzzle 135, the interval contains 2134 candidates, while a generic square-root search is on the order of 267 curve operations, before implementation-specific factors. That is an enormous reduction, though still a formidable computation.

Important distinctions

This is not mining and it does not break Bitcoin.

Not Bitcoin mining

Mining repeatedly hashes block headers to secure the network and order transactions. Puzzle hunting tests private keys against a fixed list of challenge addresses.

Not an ordinary wallet attack

Normal Bitcoin keys are not advertised as belonging to one of these small, numbered intervals. The challenge deliberately discards almost all of the normal private-key space.

Not proof of a backdoor

A solved puzzle demonstrates that a constrained interval can eventually be searched. It does not reveal a shortcut for reversing ordinary Bitcoin addresses or public keys.

A recent example

What happened with Puzzle 135?

Puzzle 135 held 13.5 BTC and had a known public key, making a square-root interval search possible. The discoverer publicly reported a five-month search using approximately 200 GPUs. On 28 July 2026, a confirmed transaction swept the puzzle address’s 13.5 BTC balance.

We independently checked the transaction, block, funding inputs, destination amount, and public key revealed by the spending signatures. The result is preserved in our permanent evidence record.

Where Bitcoin Puzzle Quest fits

An independent, offline playground.

Bitcoin Puzzle Quest is a Mathricks app for exploring this public challenge. It generates candidate keys locally on an iPhone or iPad, derives their Bitcoin addresses, and checks them against an offline copy of the published puzzle list.

Mathricks did not create the Bitcoin Puzzle, is not affiliated with its anonymous creator, and does not control, sponsor, or administer its funds. Candidate keys and matches stay on your device.

The remaining intervals are astronomically large. The app is best understood as an educational cryptography game that makes the scale of private-key searches tangible, not as an expectation of financial reward.

Common questions

Bitcoin Puzzle FAQ.

Why is it called a puzzle if there is no riddle?

The challenge asks participants to recover an unknown private key from a defined numerical interval. It is closer to a cryptographic search problem than a traditional riddle, but the “Bitcoin Puzzle” name became the community shorthand.

Is a BTC puzzle the same thing as Bitcoin mining?

No. Mining helps secure Bitcoin by hashing block headers. Puzzle hunting derives addresses from candidate private keys and compares them with a published target.

Does solving one mean Bitcoin has been cracked?

No. The puzzle keys were deliberately restricted to public, much smaller intervals. Ordinary wallet keys do not come with that advantage.

What happens when someone solves a funded puzzle?

The matching private key can authorize a transaction from that address. Any practical claim depends on the address still holding funds and on the solver safely managing the key and transaction. Mathricks does not administer the funds.
